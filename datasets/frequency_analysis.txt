Frequency analysis is the study of the frequency of letters or groups of letters in a ciphertext.
The method is used as an aid to breaking classical ciphers. In the English language, the letter E
is the most common letter, followed by T, A, O, I, N, S, H, and R. By analyzing the frequency
of characters in a ciphertext, a cryptanalyst can determine patterns that help reveal the
underlying plaintext. This technique was first documented by the Arab scholar Al Kindi in the
ninth century. Frequency analysis works particularly well against monoalphabetic substitution
ciphers. The distribution of letters in a sufficiently long ciphertext will closely mirror the
distribution of the language in which the plaintext was written.
