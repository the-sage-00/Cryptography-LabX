The Vigenere cipher is a method of encrypting alphabetic text by using a series of interwoven
Caesar ciphers based on the letters of a keyword. Though the cipher is easy to understand and
implement, for three centuries it resisted all attempts to break it. It was first described by
Giovan Battista Bellaso in 1553 but was later misattributed to Blaise de Vigenere. The cipher
earned the description of being unbreakable, le chiffre indechiffrable. Friedrich Kasiski was
the first to publish a general method of deciphering a Vigenere cipher in 1863. The key in a
Vigenere cipher is a series of letters used to shift the plaintext. Each letter in the key
determines the shift for the corresponding letter in the plaintext. If the key is shorter than
the message, it is repeated to match the length of the plaintext.
